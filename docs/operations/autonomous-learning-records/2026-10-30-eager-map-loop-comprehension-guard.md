## Material failure — prospective iterator-driven guard bypass

- **Symptom:** A `for` loop or list/set/dict comprehension iterating `map(sys.exit, [0])` can terminate before the canonical CI guard while the protected guard call and module entrypoint remain syntactically present. The same constructs over `map(exec, ["main = None"])` can rebind `main` before a Python prewrite candidate's entry guard.
- **Observed evidence:** At reviewed source `33e56a53000015a720d3a99f31a691e9099c37ba`, the AST visitors recognized eager builtin consumers of map but not `ast.For`, `ast.AsyncFor` or eager comprehension iteration over a mapped callable. A standalone generator expression merely constructs a lazy generator and should not be rejected as eager consumption. This is a prospective static gap, not an observed CI compromise or production incident.
- **Verified root cause:** The visitor traversed `map` construction without modeling iteration as the execution point for the mapped callable.
- **Failed approach:** Treating `list(map(...))`/builtin consumers as the complete eager map execution surface, or treating generator expression construction as eager iteration.
- **Correction / executable recurrence guard:** [`2cb34a3a`](https://github.com/m-shogo/memories-project/commit/2cb34a3a73e9b5c3f9ed6768f44cfebf2f9f2319) adds bounded lexical detection of direct map iterators in `for`/`async for` and list/set/dict comprehensions to both the prewrite main-binding and CI guard reachability validators. Four exact-diagnostic canonical negatives cover module/main loop and comprehension execution; two nested prewrite negatives cover mapped exec. Two positive controls preserve deferred generator expressions across both validators.
- **Retry condition:** Reconsider legitimate eager map iteration only with reviewed callable/iteration semantics, changed preconditions, bounded prewrite, exact-source CI, expected diagnostic, CAS and protected-authority verification.
