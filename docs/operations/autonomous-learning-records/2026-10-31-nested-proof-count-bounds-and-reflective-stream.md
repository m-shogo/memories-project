## Material failure mode: noncanonical or oversized nested PASS count

- **symptom:** The nested learning-guard PASS classifier could accept a zero-padded negative-case count, and a syntactically decimal count of 5,000 digits could raise a Python `ValueError` rather than returning a fail-closed classification. This is a verified latent classifier failure mode, not an observed production or CI compromise.
- **evidence:** At source `75f7518a`, `learning_guard_passed` used `count.isascii() and count.isdecimal() and int(count) >= MIN_LEARNING_NEGATIVE_CASES` with no length or canonical spelling check. Python's integer-string conversion rejects 5,000 decimal digits at the default 4,300-digit limit; `000601` previously met the numeric floor. The reviewed case floor was 601.
- **rootCauseOrUnknown:** Verified: unbounded integer conversion of untrusted child proof text and numeric-only comparison without a canonical decimal representation. No production/root operational cause is inferred.
- **failedApproach:** Treating ASCII decimal text plus numeric minimum as a complete proof grammar, without guarding integer conversion limits or leading zeroes.
- **correction:** [Code commit 0a0e4956](https://github.com/m-shogo/memories-project/commit/0a0e4956e3558edae5bc956a3cd8bddd1e204445) requires a nonempty, non-zero-prefixed, ASCII-decimal count of at most nine digits before converting it to an integer; the existing 601-case floor and future expanded positive control are preserved.
