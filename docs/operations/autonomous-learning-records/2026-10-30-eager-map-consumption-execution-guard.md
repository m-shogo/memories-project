## Material failure — prospective eager iterator-consumption bypass

- **Symptom:** `list(map(sys.exit, [0]))`, `next(map(sys.exit, [0]))` or `any(map(sys.exit, [0]))` can execute a terminating callable before the canonical CI guard despite the correct guard AST remaining present. `list(map(exec, ["main = None"]))` can similarly rebind `main` before a Python prewrite candidate's live entry guard.
- **Observed evidence:** At reviewed source `063181b64fb7e53833a5723b600580ceae55635e`, the AST visitors recognized direct and reflective calls, callable factories, partial and operator.call dispatch, but did not distinguish lazy `map` construction from its eager consumption by builtins. The prior exact-negative inventory lacked mapped-callable consumer cases. This is a prospective static gap, not observed CI compromise or a production incident.
- **Verified root cause:** The outer `list`/`next`/`any` AST callee does not identify the function invoked by the inner `map` iterator when consumed.
- **Failed approach:** Treating the absence of a direct `sys.exit(...)` or `exec(...)` call as proof that an eager iterator consumer cannot invoke those functions; indiscriminately rejecting lazy map construction would be incorrect.
- **Correction / executable recurrence guard:** [`61803e79`](https://github.com/m-shogo/memories-project/commit/61803e79b0e7d77b2d5a474f861b079f211f89fb) conservatively rejects direct `map(...)` arguments to the reviewed eager consumers `list`, `tuple`, `set`, `any`, `all`, `sum` and `next` in both CI reachability and prewrite module-binding checks. Four canonical exact-diagnostic negatives cover module and main-body termination, plus two nested prewrite negatives for mapped `exec`. Four positive controls preserve standalone lazy map construction and deferred function-body consumption across the two validators.
- **Retry condition:** Legitimate eager mapped calls require reviewed callable binding, iteration semantics and execution order, changed source preconditions, bounded prewrite/CI checks, exact-source success, CAS and protected-authority verification. This does not prove arbitrary iterator consumers, dynamic aliases, transitive execution or complete Go/Python input closure.
